# The forgotten machines: NASA hardware left on the Moon and Mars

Written by Natalie Walker

Checked on 2026-11-14. Each row cites a NASA page read on that date. Rows or cells marked **not verified** must not be stated as fact in the app. The app's two sites are:

- **Tranquility Base** (Apollo 11): 0.674°N, 23.473°E
- **Jezero Crater** (Perseverance): 18.44°N, 77.45°E

Coordinates are given as each source publishes them. Mars longitudes from NASA GISS Mars24 are in degrees east (°E). The NASA Science Viking 1 page uses degrees west (°W).

## Strongest rows for the story

- **Tranquility Base:** rows 1–3. The lander stage, the seismometer and the laser mirror all stand within a few tens of metres of each other.
  - The mirror is still working 50+ years later.
  - The seismometer fell silent within weeks.
  - That contrast is a ready-made "abandoned but not forgotten" beat. This is an Interpretation about storytelling, not a source claim.
- **Jezero:** rows 9 and 10. Ingenuity photographed the gear Perseverance threw away, and then became a left-behind machine itself.
- **Dust as the reason machines go quiet:** rows 14, 15 and 17 (Spirit, Opportunity, InSight). This links the dust lesson on the Moon to Mars.

## Gaps

- No verified image yet of the Apollo 11 descent stage or of LRO views of Tranquility Base.
- Whether the Lunar Roving Vehicles remain on the Moon (row 7) needs a NASA source.
- Coordinates for Ingenuity's final airfield and the backshell were not captured.
- NASA's 2011 lunar historic-sites guidance (PDF linked in `nasa-resources.md`, section 2) could not be read. It is the most on-theme NASA document for this table and should be checked.
